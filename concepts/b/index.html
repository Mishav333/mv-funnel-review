I · The verdict

The marketing is working. What happens after it isn’t.

Halden’s ads do their job. people clicked through, and one in ten asked for a quote. For a cold audience and a considered purchase, that is a healthy page and a healthy offer.

The money disappears after the lead arrives. Most quote requests are never booked in for an assessment, and many that are booked never happen. By the time a proposal lands, the team is working with a fraction of what the ads delivered. Once they are in the room, they close well.

So the first fix is not creative, targeting or budget. It is the first hour after someone raises their hand.

IV · The plan, with its costs

Three changes. Each one has a price, and we have paid it in the forecast.

How we forecast. Most growth plans list what a change buys and leave out what it costs. Cheaper leads arrive colder. Faster callbacks need a person. We charge each cost to the stage where it lands, before any outcome is shown.

VI · The next 90 days

Fix the first hour. Then spend more.

Weeks 1 to 3

Answer faster.

A callback role in place, so every quote request is called inside the hour. A reminder sequence on every booked assessment.

Weeks 4 to 8

Widen carefully.

Broaden the audience in one controlled test. Judge it on cost per attended assessment, never on cost per lead.

Weeks 9 to 12

Scale what pays.

Add spend only once advertising sits under 40% of job profit. Until then, more budget buys more leak.
