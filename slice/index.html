The fixes, priced honestly

Three levers. Every one pays a tax.

01

Call every quote request within the hour.

What it buys

More quote requests turn into booked assessments. The hour-one rate is 59%. The next-day rate is 27%. This lever moves people from the second queue into the first.

The tax it pays

A dedicated callback role. $3,000 across the period, added to cost in every scenario below. Nothing here is priced as free.

02

Remind before every assessment.

What it buys

Fewer empty driveways. Show rate moves from 56% to between 60 and 68%. That is the $6,431 gate closing most of the way.

The tax it pays

No extra media. Run by the same callback role, already costed in lever one.

03

Broaden the audience, last.

What it buys

20 to 40% more quote requests for the same media spend, once the funnel can hold what it catches.

The tax it pays

Colder leads book less often. We take 2 to 5 points off the booking rate to pay for it, and that tax is in every forecast.

No lever is free. A plan that prices one as free is lying to you.

The first 90 days

Fix the funnel first. Then feed it.

Days 1 to 14

Turn the lights on

The callback role goes live: every quote request called inside the hour, reminders before every assessment. Levers one and two, $3,000 for the period, no new media.

Days 15 to 45

Hold and measure

Weekly booking rate and show rate against the 59% hour-one benchmark. No new spend until both hold for four straight weeks. If they slip, the problem is operations, not media, and buying more leads would only make it dearer.

Days 46 to 90

Widen the net

Broaden the audience and take the booking-rate tax knowingly. Target the expected future: 25 signed at $1,656 each, with the conservative case as the floor the plan must clear.
